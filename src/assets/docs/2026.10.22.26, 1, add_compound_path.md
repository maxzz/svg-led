**What it does**

- **Detection:** a `<path>` is a compound path when its `d` has two or more subpaths. Your example counts as two: an open outline and a closed inner loop.
- **Make compound path:** available with two or more selected objects that share a parent, either at the top level or inside the same group. Paths and native shapes are joined; shapes are converted to paths first. Groups and mixed parents show the item disabled.
- **Release compound path:** available when a selected path is compound. Each subpath becomes its own path with the same attributes. The first keeps the `id`, and the rest drop it so ids stay unique.
- **Where it appears:** the Object menu and command palette, the canvas right-click menu, and the Actions row in the properties panel.

**Choices you may want to change**

- **Attributes and stacking:** the combined path keeps the attributes and transform of the topmost object and sits at its position in the stack. I believe that matches Illustrator, but I didn't verify it against Illustrator.
- **Transforms:** the other paths' geometry is moved into the topmost path's coordinates, so their own transforms are baked in.
- **Output format:** the resulting `d` is written in normalized absolute form, as other transforms in this app already do.
- **Released holes:** releasing your example gives two separate paths, so the inner loop becomes a filled shape rather than a hole.
- **Shortcuts:** I didn't add any keyboard shortcuts.
